## Description

Risograph Print builds a tactile graphic identity from limited spot-color layers, translucent overprint, slight registration drift, coarse halftone grain, flat shapes, and visible paper fiber. It suits lyric videos, album visuals, rhythmic graphic MVs, and projects seeking handmade editorial energy; compared with cleaner screen-print graphics, it favors duplicator-like ink variation, porous texture, and deliberately unstable layer alignment.

## Analysis

**Print Process & Form:** The image is constructed from a small set of stencil-like ink layers. Shapes stay flat, simplified, and clearly separated so overlaps and registration shifts remain visible parts of the design.

**Color System:** A limited group of vivid spot colors creates secondary hues through transparent overprint rather than continuous digital gradients. Ink density varies slightly across broad areas, keeping color graphic but materially uneven.

**Composition & Graphic Space:** Bold cropped forms, clear negative space, asymmetrical editorial balance, and repeated geometric rhythms organize the frame. Depth comes from overlapping color plates and scale changes rather than modeled perspective or cinematic lighting.

**Texture & Finish:** Coarse halftone dots, soft ink edges, minor smudging, visible paper fibers, and small registration offsets keep the surface tactile. Imperfections remain controlled enough that the graphic hierarchy stays legible.

**Transfer & Boundary:** The grammar transfers across abstract forms, objects, environments, and simplified figures as long as the limited color separations, overprint behavior, and duplicator texture remain primary. It should not collapse into clean vector minimalism, photoreal printing, or generic vintage paper noise.
